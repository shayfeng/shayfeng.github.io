Selected Publications

IROS 2026First Author · Accepted

TactileReflex: Noise-Statistics-Driven Vision-Tactile Reflex Control for Force-Sensitive Manipulation

Ziyan Feng, Yulong Fu, Zheng Li, Yuxin He, Jieji Ren, Yudong Zhong, Lujia Wang, Jinni Zhou, and Qiang Nie

Uses intrinsic sensor noise to self-calibrate tactile control thresholds. Three prioritized reflexes regulate slip, excess grip, and overload without external force calibration, material models, or manual threshold tuning.

Dynamic pouring: 9/10 vs. 0/10 with fixed grip. Prevented irreversible deformation in 5/5 trials.

CoRL 2026First Author · Accepted

What is the Better Curriculum: Controller-Shaped Grasping Behavior for Contact Force-Sensitive Manipulation

Ziyan Feng et al.

Uses a high-rate tactile controller as a teacher during demonstration collection. Tactile-free ACT and π0.5 students learn safer force-sensitive grasping with unchanged inference inputs.

Stable grasping: ACT 15% → 95%; π0.5 5% → 95%. Disturbance tests distinguish learned nominal behavior from the fast feedback needed for recovery.

Current Research

Ongoing

How can a VLA policy acquire missing physical information, adapt during contact, and use interaction history in later decisions?

  1. Active physical perception

    Probe before acting: lift, squeeze, micro-slide, or tilt to resolve visually ambiguous mass, stiffness, friction, and center of mass.

  2. Fast physical adaptation

    Couple semantic VLA control with high-rate tactile residuals or reflexes for contact stabilization, slip recovery, and force-safe execution.

  3. Physical-aware generalist VLA

    Explore physical memory and event-triggered re-grounding to guide probing, regrasping, force adjustment, and replanning.
